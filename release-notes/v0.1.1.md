GS1 follows three more behaviours of real clients, from the 2026-11-13 labs: `selectedweapon` writes, bare-number loop conditions and `timeout` rounding.

## Scripting

- **`selectedweapon` can be written.** In GS1 lowered to GS2, `selectedweapon = n` and `selectedweapon++` keep the GS2 name `selectedweapon`.
  - On a real 5.24r4 client, `= 1`, `= 0` and `++` each read back as written, with no script error. Seihai's bytecode and the server's gs2-parser compile gave the same results.
  - This brings back the Era scripts that #21 had refused for `selectedweapon` writes.
- **Flag writes stay refused.** On the same client, a GS2 `player.<flag> = ...` sends no flag packet (PLI 18/19) and is gone after a relogin. The error now says so. (#30)
- **A bare-number loop condition is false.** `while (1)`, `while (0.5)` and `for (;1;)` never ran their body on 1.41 rev 1, 2.05r2, 2.17.1, 2.22 and 2.31, because the client reads a bare number as a flag name. `while ((0+1))` did run. `if (1)` already worked this way. (Graal_analysis #442)
- **GS1 `timeout` rounds down from 1.41.** On 1.41 rev 1, 2.05r2, 2.17.1 and 2.31, a timeout counts in whole 50 ms steps, rounded down:
  - `0.01` and `0.03` never fire.
  - `0.05` and `0.07` fire every 50 ms.
  - `0.1` and `0.12` fire every 100 ms.
  - The C++ engine (3.04, 3.153 to 4.20) already did this. 1.21 to 1.39 and 5.x/6.x were not measured, so they keep the round-up to one tick. (Graal_analysis #443)
